Support

Eatlas turns a photo of your plate into calories and macros, and keeps your food diary on your iPhone.

Need help? Write to storschajnikita@gmail.com. Include your iPhone model and iOS version. You will get a reply within a few days.

Requirements

An iPhone with iOS 26.2 or later. Photo analysis needs an internet connection. The weekly coach needs an iPhone with Apple Intelligence; on other iPhones everything else works, and Insights says Apple Intelligence is unavailable.

Questions

How many photos can I analyze?

Three meals a day. A photo with no food in it doesn't use one. The count starts again every day at midnight UTC. Barcode scanning and logging a meal again have no limit and keep working after you reach it.

How accurate are the numbers?

They are estimates. Portion size is the hardest part to judge from a photo, so check the weights on the review screen and change anything that looks wrong before you save. For packaged food, the barcode gives the label's numbers.

The photo says "No food in that one".

Take the photo from above, in good light, with the whole plate in the frame. If the food is packaged, scan its barcode instead.

The barcode isn't found.

Eatlas looks barcodes up in Open Food Facts, a database built by volunteers, and some products are not in it yet. Take a photo of the food instead.

How are my daily targets worked out?

From your sex, age, height, weight, activity and goal, with the Mifflin–St Jeor formula. They update when you change your profile, and you can set your own numbers in the Me tab.

Where is the weekly coach?

It appears in Insights after you log at least three meals over two days in a week, on iPhones with Apple Intelligence turned on.

The camera doesn't open.

Allow camera access in Settings › Apps › Eatlas › Camera. You can also pick a photo from your library without camera access.

Where is my data, and how do I delete it?

Your diary and profile stay on your iPhone. Delete a meal from its detail screen; deleting the app removes everything. See the privacy policy for what a photo analysis sends.
